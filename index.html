Everything I share, I've lived.

I've worn a lot of hats, some by choice and some because life handed them to me. So when I give advice or teach, it comes from things I've actually done, plus the courses I keep taking because I'm never done learning. Every lesson here has a story behind it.

Growing up

The girl with the Olympus

Picture a Metro Manila summer. The sun is out, the street is loud, and a group of kids is deep in a game of tumbang preso. I was one of them, usually outside longer than I was allowed. I grew up in a couple of cities here, in a home full of love and laughter, and I played every Filipino street game we had a name for: piko, Chinese garter, tagu‑taguan, habulan.

I was training in badminton by kindergarten, racket almost as tall as me. Volleyball came next, then basketball, because my dad loves the game and in our house that was reason enough.

Then, in elementary, I got my first digital camera, an Olympus, and it rarely left my hand. I filmed everything: the plants, my friends, every summer. When I wasn't filming, I was reading. My mom is a bookworm, so Nancy Drew was always within reach, and everyone who knew me knew a book was the safest gift.

I didn't know it yet, but that girl with the Olympus was already doing my job. Notice the small things. Turn them into a story. Share it.

Junior high, 2012 to 2016

Journalist and student athlete

Junior high is where my stories found paper. At Mater Carmeli School of Novaliches, I joined The Carmelian, our official English publication, along with the English Club and the MAPEH Club. When I wasn't writing, I was on the court: varsity badminton in Grade 9 and Grade 10, the same sport I'd been chasing since kindergarten.

At 15, I wanted a corner of the internet that was entirely mine, so I built one. Voice From Within, my WordPress blog, ran for five years with reflections, travel stories, culture and reviews. My first website. My first audience. My first taste of hitting publish and waiting to see who reads.

And I found my people. The friends I bonded with over anime, K dramas and long afternoons after class are still my closest friends today.

Senior high, 2016 to 2018

Sports editor and campus TV correspondent

Senior high at San Sebastian College‑Recoletos Manila, Humanities and Social Sciences strand, and suddenly my byline had a home. I started at The Sebastinian as a contributor, and my first published piece covered the school's production of Cinderella. By 2018, I was Sports Editor.

Then I picked up a microphone. As a correspondent for Bravo TV, San Sebastian's campus channel, I covered our NCAA games courtside: basketball, volleyball, swimming, table tennis and, of course, badminton, plus everything happening around campus. I even had my own segment, One on One with Andrea Galvan, where I sat down with our athletes for interviews. The kid with the Olympus was now the one asking the questions.

That same year, Magic 89.9, the FM radio station, invited me into its Street Team, an invite only community built around personality, confidence and community. Two sessions stayed with me long after the lights came up: resiliency and mental health with Doc Gia Sison, host of CNN Philippines GTalks, and Storytelling 101 with Serge Gabriel, a speaker who can hold an entire room in the palm of his hand. I took notes. I still think about that room.

College, 2019 to 2023

Communicator and events host

College at Centro Escolar University Manila meant a Bachelor of Arts in Communication and Media, a seat in the CEU Communication and Media Society, and a lot more time on stage than I expected.

Somehow I became the person holding the program together. I hosted the Freshmen Party in 2019, and that October, Mug Talk @ CEU, an open forum by our Communication and Media Program where the panel took on one question: "Donya Ada sa Pelikula? Bakit Hindi!" In 2021, I hosted our Communication and Media Acquaintance Party. Different crowds, same job: keep the room with you.

2021

A stage in Nueva Ecija

In the middle of the pandemic, I traveled to Nueva Ecija. The Agricultural Training Institute had invited me as a resource speaker for its Binhi ng Pag‑asa Program, and my audience was a room of young farmers and agripreneurs. We talked about the state of Philippine agriculture, leadership and values formation, and how social media can work for a farm. It taught me that a good story travels anywhere, far beyond the city.

2023 to 2025

Learning the business from the inside

Next came the side of the work most people never see. As a Junior Account Manager at In A Jiffy Production Company, I handled clients in finance, media, entertainment, hospitality and F&B, ran brand and store launches, and supervised video shoots from call time to wrap.

At DForce Operations Center, I became a Social Media Content Creator for small and medium businesses across Facebook, Instagram, X, Pinterest and YouTube. Planning six months of content at a time teaches you patience. It also teaches you systems, and I fell for both.

2025 to mid 2026

A first nudge from AI

In 2025, I stepped up as Social Media Lead at RepublicAsia Media, running the daily content team and reporting every week on what worked and what to change. That same year, my role was handed to AI. Yes, really.

You'd think that would send me straight into learning AI. Not yet. I moved on to Kai Mall as Social Media Specialist, where a Reels first strategy took Kai Inan Food Town from paid reach to organic growth. Then came brand operations at All‑Natural Goodness Corporation, running ecommerce across Shopee, Lazada and TikTok Shop and managing creator campaigns. Through all of it, AI was still just a smarter search bar to me. That was about to change.

July and August 2026

The turn

In July 2026, I quit my nine to five. August was a blur of applications and interviews, and every single interviewer asked me the same thing: how do you use AI? Every single time, I gave the same answer, and it was only the basics. The offers never came.

So I did what I've always done. I adapted. I wanted to earn, so I went freelance, and this time I took AI seriously. My skills grew ten times over that season. Everything you see after this chapter started right there.

September to November 2026

Freelancer, then founder

In September 2026, I started freelancing, and the clients came: from the Philippines, the US and Australia, from growing brands to founders building their personal brands. They came for the AI systems I was now building every single day.

In November, my birth month, I gave all of it a home and founded North Bloom Collective, a creative and technology venture studio. A birthday gift to myself, you could say.

North for the north side of Metro Manila, where I grew up. Bloom for the flowers I love, preferably given while I'm still around to enjoy them. Collective for the people who tend the garden with me, toward a shared purpose. And burgundy with coffee browns, because that is me: warm, a little fiery, and fueled by coffee. Why North Bloom Collective

North

I grew up on the north side of Metro Manila. Every bloom starts with its roots, and North is mine. It keeps where I come from in everything I build, no matter how far it grows.

Bloom

I only realized how much I love flowers when a date asked if I'd like to receive them. My quick answer: yes, and I'd rather get them now, while I'm alive to enjoy them, than at my funeral. Smooth? Maybe not. True? Completely. A bloom is growth you can see, and that is what I want for every brand and every person I work with, from companies to personal brands.

Collective

A collective is a garden. No single flower makes it; people tend it together, each with a role, toward a common goal. Some plant, some water, some pull the weeds, and everyone shares the harvest when it comes. That is the spirit I want North Bloom to carry, and an open invitation to clients, creators and partners to grow something meaningful with us.

The colors

Burgundy and maroon are my personality: warm, but also fiery and feisty. The brown tones come from my love of coffee.
